# Plan of Attack B Side Presentation Plan

Status: Implemented for local review. Publication pending review.

Present The Dionysus Program and Plan of Attack as two sides of one publication. The identity stays fixed while the reader's position changes: the book addresses the organization that needs to change; the letters address the founder who can make its delay expensive.

## Entry and navigation

Place an A/B selector immediately beneath the existing book cover, giving Plan of Attack a more prominent relationship to the book than another item among the companion links:

- A — The Dionysus Program
- B — Plan of Attack

Use the selector itself to switch sides, without separate flip links beneath either cover. Retain the selector on both sides and restore the reader's position when returning to Side A. Give the letters their own address and individual letter anchors so readers can share or enter them directly.

## Visual identity

The family resemblance comes from the existing stone cover, classical lettering, Φ mark, serif typography, and generous reading margins. Side B should feel like the same stone after dark.

| Element | Side B treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Background | Warm charcoal with an almost imperceptible stone texture |
| Text | Soft ivory |
| Typography | The same serif family and reading proportions |
| Identity | The Dionysus Program masthead and Φ mark |
| Accent | Muted antique gold, used sparingly |
| Composition | Spare, with stronger contrasts in type size |

Silenus supplies the menace; the design remains composed.

## The flip

When activated, the cover briefly turns through its edge to reveal the B-side title treatment, and the surrounding page changes tone. Confine the motion to the cover area and honor reduced-motion preferences. After the transition, provide ordinary, comfortable reading. The record metaphor lives in the gesture, A/B labeling, and sequence of six letters.

## Opening treatment

Proposed display copy:

> THE DIONYSUS PROGRAM  
> SIDE B
>
> **PLAN OF ATTACK**
>
> Six letters to a founder  
> whose company does not yet exist.
>
> *From Silenus.*
>
> The organizations reading the other side are trying to repair themselves. These letters concern what you can do before they finish.
>
> **Read Letter I ↓**

Keep Sean Devine's authorship clear in a quieter credit. Silenus is the fictional correspondent.

## Reading the letters

Use a compact contents list showing I–VI with the current rallying cries. Keep the letters on one continuous page, with an individual link to each.

Within each letter, use a small Roman numeral and title for position, give the rallying cry the strongest typography, set the vulnerability-and-attack paragraph slightly larger, and let the body settle into comfortable reading. Preserve the salutation and give “Yours, Silenus” space to land. Use the canonical letter text in `content/plan-of-attack.md` when implementing; do not maintain a second copy of the prose in this plan.

## Return to Side A

After the last signature:

> **Read the manual your adversary needs.**  
> Flip to Side A →

A founder can discover the ideas behind the attacks. An incumbent can return to the book with a more personal reason to read it.
