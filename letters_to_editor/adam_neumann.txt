To the Editor of the Dionysus Program,

You put WeWork under Internal Collapse and call it The Overwhelmed. Your explanation is that growth exceeded the organization’s ability to sustain trust. I want to defend what that account leaves out.

People joined because they wanted to build something larger than an office-leasing business.

You describe the Dionysian as intoxication and loss of boundaries, then require Apollo to contain it. My objection is that some possibilities only become visible after people commit beyond what looks reasonable.


You call excess demand on the organization “Overflow.” I saw it as pressure to build capacity we did not yet have. The mistake may have been the pace or the financing. It does not follow that the ambition was wrong.

We had shared experience and strong loyalty. Summer Camp mattered to people. Your account needs to explain why those bonds did not translate into an organization that could sustain the commitments we made.

I am less persuaded by the Cincinnatus Rule. If a founder supplies the direction that holds the company together, removing that person may weaken it. You need a way to distinguish dependence that should end from leadership that still contributes.


Your program is clearer about survival than about what makes a large ambition worth attempting. A company can obey every limit and still build nothing of consequence.

That is the risk I would ask you to weigh alongside the risk of overextension.


Not Adam Neumann
